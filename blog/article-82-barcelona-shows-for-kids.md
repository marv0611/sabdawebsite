Finding Barcelona shows for kids as a visiting family comes down to three questions: will my child understand it without speaking Catalan or Spanish, will they last the whole thing, and what will it cost for all of us? This guide answers those three for every show and exhibition worth planning a family day around in October and November 2026, from immersive rooms and magic shows to sharks, chocolate and a Halloween village.

Every price, age and date below was checked on each venue's official website on 28 September 2026. One of the venues, SABDA, is ours, and we say so where it appears.

---

## First, the language question

Most theatre in Barcelona is performed in Catalan or Spanish, which matters when your children only speak English. The safest picks for visiting families are shows where the story is visual: immersive rooms, magic, dance, aquariums and science museums. Where a show depends on language, we say which one.

## Immersive shows and exhibitions for kids

### SABDA's new immersive show (Eixample), from 17 October

Full disclosure: SABDA is our venue. SABDA's new immersive show is a 25 minute journey through four worlds of life on Earth, made in Unreal Engine and projected across the four walls of a 360° room with Dolby Atmos sound, for 25 people per session at C/ Muntaner 83B in the Eixample.

It opened on 17 October 2026 and runs on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays. Tickets are €20 for adults and €12 for children, including a welcome glass of cava or a juice in SABDA Café. The journey happens at animal eye level, so for 25 minutes you become one of the animals. Nobody wears a headset. The nearest metro is Universitat. Book on [SABDA's exhibitions page](/exhibitions/), since each session holds 25 people.

**Why it works for kids:** 25 minutes is about as long as a young child's attention lasts, the child ticket is €12, and there is nothing to put on their head.

### Antarctica Experience at GIGA

Antarctica Experience is an immersive exhibition about the Antarctic inside the dome of the Arenas building at Plaça d'Espanya. Tickets start at €12.90, under-3s go free, and it is open to all ages. A visit takes 45 to 75 minutes.

### Casa Batlló

Children up to 12 go free at Casa Batlló on Passeig de Gràcia, and they get an augmented reality tablet that turns Gaudí's house into a treasure hunt. The visit takes about 1 hour 15 minutes and ends in the Gaudí Cube, a six-sided LED room. Adult tickets start at €29.

### Codi Gaudí at Espai Imagina

Codi Gaudí is a mixed and virtual reality exhibition about the buildings Gaudí never built, at Av. Diagonal 177, until 8 January 2027. It uses VR headsets, which the venue recommends from age 6. Child tickets (ages 4 to 12) cost €12.50 on Mondays, €13.50 Wednesday to Friday and €18.50 at weekends; under-3s go free. It takes about 70 minutes and is closed on Tuesdays.

### IKONO and Moco Museum

IKONO, on the third floor of the Arenas shopping centre, is a playground of more than ten photo-friendly immersive rooms, open daily from 12:00 to 19:00. A child ticket costs €12.90 on Fever, the seller IKONO links to, and under-16s must come with an adult. Moco Museum in the Born has digital and mirror rooms alongside Banksy and KAWS; tickets start at €14.95 and children up to 6 go free.

### Iniesta Experience, for football fans

Iniesta Experience: The Inner Game runs in the former IMAX on Moll d'Espanya until 10 January 2027, daily from 10:00 to 20:00. It lasts about 90 minutes and tickets start at €18. The venue does not publish a child price, so it suits older children and teenagers best.

## Family theatre, magic and music

**El Mago Pop, "Nada es imposible".** The illusionist's new show plays the Teatre Victòria from 14 October 2026 to 31 January 2027, from €42. The show is in Spanish, but most of it is visual. It is not recommended for under-3s, every person needs a ticket, and it uses flashing lights and lasers. Many dates are already sold out, so book early. [teatrevictoria.com](https://www.teatrevictoria.com/es/cartelera/c/332-el-mago-pop.html)

**SAT! Sant Andreu Teatre.** A family theatre with shows of about an hour at €10.50. For visitors, the best bet is "Natzuri" on 25 October, a dance piece with no text for ages 5 and up. "En Joan sense por i la casa encantada", from 31 October to 8 November for ages 4 and up, is in Catalan.

**Palau de la Música Catalana.** Family concerts in the Modernista concert hall that is a UNESCO World Heritage Site: "Twinkle, twinkle, baby's star" for ages 0 to 5 on 24, 25 and 31 October, "Let's fly with Tchaikovsky" for ages 6 and up on 8 November, and "Flamenkat" for ages 6 and up on 22 November. [palaumusica.cat](https://www.palaumusica.cat/en/programming-and-tickets_1192313)

**L'Auditori.** "Monstres", a concert for ages 2 and up, on 7 and 8 November (€11), and "Toy Box" with the Barcelona Symphony Orchestra, for ages 5 and up, on 28 November (€13).

**Los Chicos del Coro.** The musical opens at the Teatre Apolo on 26 November 2026, rated for all audiences, from €34. At 135 minutes, it suits school-age children rather than toddlers.

## Science, sharks and animals

**CosmoCaixa.** Barcelona's science museum is €8 for adults and free for under-16s, and opens daily from 10:00 to 20:00. Clik is a play space for ages 3 to 11 (€6 on top of entry), and a new temporary exhibition, "Tiburones. Los guardianes del océano", about sharks, opens on 22 October 2026. It is easily a half-day visit. [cosmocaixa.org](https://cosmocaixa.org/es/familia)

**L'Aquàrium de Barcelona.** Open every day of the year in Port Vell from 10:00. Adults (11 and over) pay €30, children aged 5 to 10 €23, children aged 3 and 4 €15, and under-3s go free. A family ticket for two adults and two children costs €88. [aquariumbcn.com](https://www.aquariumbcn.com/planificar-visita/precios/)

**Museu Blau.** The natural sciences museum at the Fòrum has whale skeletons, minerals and a temporary exhibition called "Animals invisibles" until 10 January 2027. Entry is €6, free for under-16s, and free for everyone on Sunday afternoons from 15:00. Closed on Mondays.

**Museu de la Xocolata.** A small chocolate museum in the Born, €8 for adults and free for under-6s. It takes less than an hour, which is exactly right for small children.

## Halloween in Barcelona with kids

**Creepy Halloween Family at Poble Espanyol.** Poble Espanyol, the open-air village of Spanish architecture on Montjuïc, runs a family Halloween on the weekends of 17 and 18 October, 24 and 25 October, and 31 October and 1 November 2026. Tickets start at €12.50, with discounts for families of three or four. It is for all ages and recommended up to 12; the haunted tunnels are gentler during the day (ages 3 and up before 17:00) and scarier later (ages 7 and up). [poble-espanyol.com](https://poble-espanyol.com/actividad/halloween-en-el-poble/)

## Barcelona with kids when it rains

Autumn in Barcelona is mostly mild, but when it rains, every venue in this guide is indoors. The trick is to group them by area so nobody gets soaked between stops.

**Plaça d'Espanya: one building, a whole afternoon.** Antarctica Experience and IKONO are both inside Arenas, so you can do both without going outside.

**Port Vell: fish and boats.** The Aquàrium is a short walk from the Museu Marítim, which is free for under-17s and free for everyone on Sunday afternoons from 15:00.

**Eixample: Gaudí and a short show.** Casa Batlló in the morning, free for under-12s, then SABDA's 25 minute immersive show in the afternoon on a Friday, Saturday or Sunday.

**A full day in one place.** CosmoCaixa, with Clik and the shark exhibition, fills a rainy day on its own and is free for under-16s.

For grown-up plans on the same wet day, see our [rainy day in Barcelona guide](/blog/rainy-day-barcelona/).

## Practical tips for visiting families

**Check the closing day.** Codi Gaudí closes on Tuesdays and the Museu Blau on Mondays. Most other venues in this guide open every day.

**Use Sunday afternoons.** From 15:00 on Sundays, several city museums, including the Museu Blau and the Museu Marítim, are free for everyone, which is useful for a family of four.

**Book the small shows first.** Anything with limited places, such as SABDA's 25-person sessions, El Mago Pop or the Palau de la Música family concerts, sells out before the big museums do.

**Plan one big thing a day.** A long museum in the morning and a short show in the afternoon works better with children than three short stops across town.

## How to keep it affordable

Barcelona is kinder to families than it first looks. CosmoCaixa and the Museu Blau are free for under-16s, the Museu Marítim for under-17s, the Picasso Museum for under-18s and Casa Batlló for children up to 12. On Sunday afternoons from 15:00, several city museums are free for everyone. The paid immersive shows cost roughly €12 to €19 per child, so the choice often comes down to how long your children can focus rather than price.

## FAQ

**What are the best Barcelona shows for kids this autumn?**
For young children, SABDA's 25 minute immersive show, CosmoCaixa and the Aquàrium. For magic, El Mago Pop at the Teatre Victòria from 14 October. For music, the family concerts at the Palau de la Música and L'Auditori. For Halloween, Creepy Halloween Family at Poble Espanyol.

**Are there shows in Barcelona for kids who don't speak Spanish or Catalan?**
Yes. Visual shows work best: immersive exhibitions such as Antarctica Experience and IKONO, El Mago Pop (in Spanish but largely visual) and "Natzuri" at SAT! Sant Andreu Teatre, a dance piece with no text.

**What can I do in Barcelona with kids when it rains?**
Group indoor venues by area: Antarctica Experience and IKONO in the same building at Plaça d'Espanya, the Aquàrium and Museu Marítim in Port Vell, or Casa Batlló and SABDA in the Eixample. CosmoCaixa can fill a whole day on its own.

**How much does SABDA's immersive show cost for children?**
A child ticket costs €12 and an adult ticket €20, including a welcome juice or glass of cava in SABDA Café. Sessions run on Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays from 17 October 2026, for 25 people each.

**Which Barcelona museums are free for children?**
CosmoCaixa and the Museu Blau are free for under-16s, the Museu Marítim for under-17s, the Picasso Museum for under-18s, and Casa Batlló for children up to 12.

**Do I need to book family shows in Barcelona in advance?**
Yes for El Mago Pop, where many dates are sold out, and for SABDA's show, which holds 25 people per session. Codi Gaudí runs timed entries every 15 minutes, so booking ahead also avoids waiting.

**Is there a Halloween event for kids in Barcelona?**
Yes. Poble Espanyol runs Creepy Halloween Family on the last October weekends and 1 November 2026, from €12.50, recommended for children up to 12.
